01

2026–present · Applied Scientist / Technical Owner · KJ group

Nationwide real-time traffic estimation

Owned the R&D and implementation of a real-time traffic-estimation system for a nationwide road network, from raw vehicle trajectories and map matching through ML-based inference and multi-source data fusion. Designed a staged Python pipeline with explicit measured, estimated, imputed and historical states. Achieved SQV > 85% on sparse FCD data.

Production-deployedClient mobility platform; actively evolving
Tools used: Python · Polars · XGBoost · Valhalla · OpenStreetMap
Expand case study problem · ownership · approach · outcome

Problem

Produce useful network-wide speed and congestion estimates from incomplete, heterogeneous floating-car data. The system had to preserve arterial-road coverage, distinguish fresh observations from fallbacks, and return results quickly enough for a live mobility product.

Ownership

I owned the traffic-estimation R&D and implementation: data preparation, map matching, feature engineering, model selection, fusion logic, testing and technical documentation. KJ Group owned the client-facing platform and its deployment infrastructure.

Approach

I separated offline preparation from the online estimation loop, using Polars and Parquet for efficient processing, Valhalla and OpenStreetMap for road-network alignment, and XGBoost for inference. The pipeline records whether each value is measured, estimated, imputed or historical, with age and confidence information instead of hiding gaps behind a single number.

Validation & outcome

The method achieved SQV above 85% on sparse FCD data and was integrated into the client mobility platform. Bus-specific correction increased usable observations, while automated tests and staged fallbacks made the production path inspectable as the system continued to evolve.

02

2025–present · Co-founder / Technical Lead · Engineering SaaS

FlowMob — automated intersection analysis

Building the simulation and calculation backend for a SaaS that automates intersection modelling, traffic simulation and standards-based capacity analysis.

Pre-commercial SaaSFunctional prototype in active development
Tools used: Python · SUMO · Microservices · Capacity analysis
Expand case study problem · ownership · approach · outcome

Problem

Early-stage intersection studies require repeated network preparation, simulation runs and capacity calculations, making comparisons slow and difficult to reproduce. FlowMob aims to turn those steps into a guided workflow for transport practitioners.

Ownership

I own the simulation, backend and calculation side of the product, including model generation, scenario execution and standards-based analysis. A separate developer owns the web frontend.

Approach

The backend exposes Python services that prepare SUMO models, run scenario sweeps and calculate capacity indicators under Hungarian engineering rules. A containerized service boundary separates the numerical workflow from the user interface and keeps individual analysis steps reproducible.

Validation & outcome

The current prototype completes the core path from intersection input through simulation to capacity results. It is being tested as a pre-commercial engineering tool; visualization, workflow refinement and formal dimensioning support remain active development areas.

2018–2019 · Research Engineer / Lead Developer for Orchestration

ZalaZONE mixed-reality digital twin

Developed the central orchestration and co-simulation layer that synchronized real vehicles with simulated traffic, virtual objects and CAN-based sensor information.

Operational demonstratorRepeated public demonstrations; not a commercial product
Tools used: Python · SUMO · TCP/IP · CAN · Unity integration
Expand case study problem · ownership · approach · outcome

Problem

Automated-driving tests need repeatable traffic situations, including rare or hazardous interactions that are costly to reproduce with physical vehicles alone. The project combined a real test vehicle and proving ground with simulated road users that remained consistent across several systems.

Ownership

I developed the central orchestration and co-simulation layer, rather than the entire platform. Partner teams owned the vehicle, visualization and other subsystem implementations; my responsibility was to coordinate their data and timing into one executable test scenario.

Approach

A Python-based master process synchronized SUMO, the visualization environment, the by-wire vehicle and physical roadside components over TCP/IP and CAN interfaces. It handled simulation time, coordinate conversion, object-state exchange and virtual sensor information for the real vehicle.

Validation & outcome

The integrated system supported repeatable mixed-reality driving scenarios and ran in multiple public demonstrations at ZalaZONE. It established an operational R&D test capability, while remaining a demonstrator rather than a commercial product.

04

2022–present · Principal Researcher · BME / National Laboratory for Autonomous Systems

Scalable mixed-reality validation

Developed and validated an adaptive traffic–vehicle co-simulation method that keeps high fidelity around the system under test while dynamically downscaling distant traffic. Implemented the traffic/vehicle multi-fidelity methods and evaluated speed–accuracy trade-offs; supervised substantial V2X extensions within the broader research programme.

Research prototypeExperimentally validated; not commercialized
Tools used: Python · SUMO / libsumo · CARLA · Co-simulation · Design of Experiments
Expand case study problem · ownership · approach · outcome

Problem

Large mixed-reality scenarios become too slow when every vehicle and interaction is simulated at maximum fidelity. The engineering target was a real-time factor above one without sacrificing the local detail needed around the vehicle or system under test.

Ownership

I directly developed the traffic and vehicle multi-fidelity methods, their implementation and the speed–accuracy evaluation. I supervised, rather than personally implemented, substantial V2X work carried out by a PhD student within the broader programme.

Approach

The method dynamically maintains a microscopic region around the ego vehicle while representing distant traffic at lower fidelity, transferring vehicles and state as the region moves. Experiments coupled SUMO/libsumo with higher-fidelity vehicle simulation and used designed parameter sweeps to quantify computational gain against modelling error.

Validation & outcome

Experiments showed a 200–500% performance improvement in CARLA–SUMO–SPM configurations while retaining high fidelity where it affected the test. The result is an experimentally validated research prototype and an open basis for larger real-time mixed-reality networks, not a commercialized platform.
